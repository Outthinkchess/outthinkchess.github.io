Chess & Academic Development

The benefits of chess can extend beyond the board. Research has found encouraging connections between chess instruction and mathematical performance in school-age children.

Denmark

Chess & Mathematics Performance

Researchers studied 482 primary-school students in Denmark. For students in the chess program, one of four weekly mathematics lessons was replaced with chess-based instruction.

Despite receiving less conventional mathematics instruction, students receiving the chess intervention subsequently demonstrated improved mathematics test performance compared with students receiving traditional mathematics instruction.

Read the Denmark Study →
Italy

Chess & Mathematical Problem-Solving

Researchers studied 560 children between the ages of 8 and 11 and examined the relationship between chess instruction and mathematical problem-solving.

The study found a strong relationship between chess ability and mathematics performance. Students receiving chess instruction also demonstrated greater improvement in mathematical problem-solving than students continuing their normal school activities.

Meet the Coach

Bledi Bejo

2170 Peak USCF • 250+ Rated Tournament Games • 2700+ Chess.com Blitz

Bledi Bejo is the founder and head coach of Outthink Chess. A lifelong competitive chess player, Bledi has played more than 250 rated over-the-board tournament games and achieved a peak USCF rating of 2170.

Online, he has reached a Chess.com blitz rating above 2700. His playing style is tactical, aggressive, and creative, with a strong emphasis on calculation, initiative, and finding opportunities others may overlook.

As a coach, Bledi's goal is not simply to teach openings and tactics. He teaches students how to think: how to analyze a position, compare alternatives, anticipate consequences, make decisions under pressure, and learn constructively from mistakes.

Outthink Chess was built around the belief that the skills developed at the chessboard can extend far beyond it — strengthening critical thinking, focus, discipline, confidence, resilience, and independent decision-making.
